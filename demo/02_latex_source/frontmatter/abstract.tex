\frontchapter{Abstract}
A fictional study of a classroom sand timer illustrates an engineering thesis.
The teaching problem is how to connect a measurable requirement to a design
choice and a transparent verification result. The objective is to compare two
imaginary timer concepts against a target duration of sixty seconds.
A simple duration model and three invented observations per concept demonstrate
the method. Concept A has a mean duration of sixty seconds, while concept B has
a mean duration of sixty-five seconds. The first concept meets the illustrative
mean-duration requirement. The small constructed dataset cannot support claims
about real devices or a wider population. Further work would require a physical
prototype, calibrated measurements and a justified sampling plan. The example
shows how sources, equations, figures and results can form a traceable argument.
All observations are fictional and exist only to teach document structure.
